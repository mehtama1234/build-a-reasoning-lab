Learning to think by imitation.

Reinforcement learning grew reasoning from scratch — powerful, but finicky, and it can cheat. Here's the other road: if a big model already reasons beautifully, have it work through thousands of problems, and simply train a small model to copy its thoughts. No reward, no discovery — just imitation. This is distillation, and it's how most small reasoning models you'll meet are actually made.

How distillation works

Copy the thoughts, not just the answers

A large, already-capable model solves a big set of problems, writing out its full <think> reasoning for each. That becomes a dataset of (question → worked solution). Then the small model is trained the ordinary way — predict the next token — on those solutions. It's plain imitation learning: no verifier, no reward, no sampling. The small model simply learns to produce the kind of step-by-step thinking the teacher produced, and that skill transfers to new problems.

The two roads, side by side

We've now built a reasoning model both ways. They trade off.

Reinforcement learning

Discovers reasoning by trial and reward. Peaked at 50% here — then collapsed to 15% without a leash. No teacher needed, but powerful and unstable; it's how the frontier teachers are trained.

Distillation

Imitates a teacher's thoughts. Climbed to 65% and stayed there — stable, simple, cheap. But it needs a strong teacher to exist first, and can't exceed what the teacher showed it.

RL creates new reasoning ability from a reward signal; distillation copies existing ability at a fraction of the cost. Real systems use both — RL to build a great teacher, distillation to spread it to small, fast models.
Why this matters — and what you've built
  • Most small reasoning models are distilled. Copying a strong reasoner's traces is the cheap, stable way to put reasoning into a model that fits on a phone.
  • Reasoning is a transferable skill, not a fixed trait. The same 0.6B network went from hopeless to a real reasoner just by being shown what thinking looks like.
  • You've now seen the whole pipeline. Prompt a model to think → measure it → sample and vote → let it self-correct → build a verifier → train with RL → distill. That's a reasoning model, end to end, on real runs.
The whole arc

We started with one flag that let a model think out loud, and ended by training reasoning into a small model two different ways. Thinking longer, thinking many times, checking itself, being graded, being rewarded, being taught — each session added one real idea, measured on a real model. A reasoning model isn't magic; it's a language model plus a scratchpad, a verifier, and a way to learn from being right. Now you've built every piece.
