Does thinking actually help?

Session 1 showed thinking rescue one problem. That could be luck. So here we do the honest thing: take 30 real grade-school math problems (GSM8K), run the same model on every one twice — answer directly, then think first — and just count how many it gets right each way.

What it cost

The accuracy wasn't free — it was bought with tokens.

This is “inference-time scaling” in one number

We spent about 6× more compute at answer-time and got +13 points of accuracy back — without touching the model's weights. That trade is the whole premise of reasoning models: you can buy quality with thinking-time, not just with training. The rest of this lab is about buying it more efficiently.

The honest part

Thinking is not a ceiling-breaker. This is a tiny 0.6B model, so even with reasoning it still misses 12 of 30 grade-school problems. And on the hardest ones it doesn't fail cleanly — 12 of 30 traces ran all the way to our 2048 token limit without ever settling on an answer, the model circling and second-guessing itself. Small reasoners ramble. The lesson isn't “reasoning makes it correct”; it's “reasoning makes it more correct, reliably, for a price” — and part of that price is a lot of wasted thinking on problems above its weight.

Where we're headed

Chain-of-thought isn't a one-problem trick — over 30 problems it's worth +13 points, paid for in about 6× the tokens. If a little thinking helps this much, what if we let the model think several times and take the answer it keeps landing on?
